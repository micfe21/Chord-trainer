🪟 Windows Setup Guide

Connect your MIDI keyboard on Windows

This guide will help you set up the Chord Trainer app with your MIDI keyboard on Windows. We recommend USB MIDI whenever possible – it's simpler, more reliable, and works immediately.

⚠️ CRITICAL – Use a DATA‑SYNC USB cable
Many USB cables are charge‑only and do NOT transmit data. If your keyboard powers on but doesn't appear in the app, try a different cable – preferably the one that came with your keyboard or one labelled for data transfer.

✅ Works: "Data‑sync", "Data transfer", "USB 2.0/3.0" cables
❌ Does NOT work: "Charge‑only", "Charging cable" (usually thin or cheap)

Option 1 – USB MIDI (Recommended)

USB MIDI is plug‑and‑play on Windows. No drivers needed for most keyboards.

  1. Plug it in – connect your keyboard to your PC using a data‑sync USB cable (not a charge‑only cable).
  2. Turn it on – power on your keyboard.
  3. Open the app in Chrome or Edge.
  4. Tap the MIDI button – your keyboard should appear in the dropdown (e.g., SMK-37 Pro Midi or USB MIDI Device).
  5. Select it – the status will change to "MIDI connected".
  6. Tap "Start Audio" and play!
✅ That's it! USB MIDI on Windows is the simplest and most reliable method.

Option 2 – Bluetooth MIDI (BLE)

Windows does not natively expose Bluetooth MIDI devices to the Web MIDI API. You need two free tools to bridge the connection:

  • loopMIDI – creates a virtual MIDI port that the app can see.
  • MIDIberry – receives Bluetooth MIDI and forwards it to the loopMIDI port.

Step 1 – Pair your keyboard

  1. Turn on your keyboard and put it in Bluetooth pairing mode (check your manual).
  2. On Windows, go to Settings → Bluetooth & devices → Add device → Bluetooth.
  3. Select your keyboard from the list (e.g., SMK-37 Pro_BLE or CASIO WU-BT10).
  4. If it asks for a PIN, try 0000 or 1234.
  5. Wait for it to show "Connected".

Step 2 – Install loopMIDI

  1. Go to loopMIDI official website.
  2. Download and install loopMIDI.
  3. Open loopMIDI.
  4. Click the "+" (Add Port) button.
  5. Give it a name, e.g., ChordTrainer Port.
  6. Leave loopMIDI running in the background.

Step 3 – Install MIDIberry

  1. Open the Microsoft Store and search for "MIDIberry".
  2. Install it (it's free).
  3. Alternatively, download from MIDIberry on Microsoft Store.

Step 4 – Configure MIDIberry

  1. Open MIDIberry.
  2. In the INPUT section, click "List All Input Devices".
  3. Find your keyboard (it should have "Bluetooth MIDI IN" in the name).
  4. Make sure the toggle next to it is ON (enabled).
  5. In the OUTPUT section, click "List All Output Devices".
  6. Find the port you created in loopMIDI (e.g., ChordTrainer Port).
  7. Enable it (toggle ON).
  8. Check the Input Monitor – when you play a key, you should see MIDI messages appearing (like NoteOn). This confirms MIDIberry is receiving your notes.
✅ At this point, MIDIberry is forwarding your keyboard's notes to the loopMIDI virtual port.

Step 5 – Connect the Chord Trainer app

  1. Open the Chord Trainer app in Chrome or Edge.
  2. Tap the MIDI button (near the top).
  3. In the dropdown that appears, select the port you created in loopMIDI (e.g., ChordTrainer Port).
  4. The status should change to "MIDI connected: ChordTrainer Port".
  5. Important: Tap the "Start Audio" orange button (this resumes the audio context on Windows).
  6. Play a note – you should hear sound and see the on‑screen keys light up.

🔧 Troubleshooting

❌ My keyboard doesn't appear in the app
→ First: try a different USB cable! This is the most common cause. Use a data‑sync cable, not a charge‑only cable.
→ Double‑tap the MIDI status text to refresh the device list.
→ Make sure your keyboard is turned on and connected.
→ Try a different USB port on your computer.
❌ No sound from the app
→ Tap the orange "Start Audio" button. This is required on Windows.
→ Make sure the Sound toggle is set to "Internal" (not "MIDI Thru").
→ Check your system volume and ensure Chrome/Edge isn't muted in the Volume Mixer.
❌ MIDIberry receives notes but the app doesn't respond
→ In the app, tap the MIDI button and double‑tap the MIDI status text to refresh the device list.
→ Make sure you selected the loopMIDI port, not your keyboard directly.
→ Check that the port is still active in loopMIDI and MIDIberry.
❌ loopMIDI port doesn't appear in MIDIberry
→ Make sure loopMIDI is running and the port is created.
→ In MIDIberry, click "List All Output Devices" again – it should appear.
→ If not, restart both applications.
